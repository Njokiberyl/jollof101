Orientation

You have finished the lessons for Section 2. Here is what to take forward into the kitchen.

Remember

Three things to remember

  1. 1
    SHARED ROOTSJollof belongs within a broader West African culinary history, with connections to the Wolof people and the Senegambian region.
  2. 2
    MANY EXPRESSIONSIngredients, rice varieties and preparation differ across countries, communities and households. There is no single recipe for every community.
  3. 3
    FOOD & IDENTITYJollof carries cultural and social meaning beyond the recipe — in families, celebrations, hospitality and national pride.

Haven’t completed your comparison yet? Go back to 2.6 Compare Two Traditions before you continue.

Looking ahead

Up next: from understanding to doing

In Section 3 you will prepare one Nigerian-style interpretation of Jollof rice. As you cook, pay attention to how the ingredients combine, how the rice absorbs the sauce, and how each stage shapes the final taste, aroma, colour and texture.

You are not simply following a recipe — you are taking part in a tradition that has evolved across generations.
