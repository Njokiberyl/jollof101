YOUR TURN: Compare two Jollof traditions

This activity measures Objective 2. Write your own comparison first — then check it against a model response.

Target: 2 accurate similarities + 2 accurate differences
Objective 2 — what this activity measures

Given two Jollof rice traditions presented in the module, the learner will compare the traditions by accurately identifying at least two similarities and two differences in their cultural context, ingredients, or preparation practices.

Success criterion: at least two accurate similarities and two accurate differences.

Reference

The two traditions

Accurate similarities include…

  • Preparation: both cook the rice in a seasoned tomato and pepper-based sauce.
  • Ingredients: both commonly use fresh tomatoes, tomato paste, onions, red bell and hot peppers, vegetable oil, garlic, ginger, thyme, curry powder and stock.
  • Cultural context: both are popular at celebrations, parties and family gatherings.
  • Cultural context: both are sources of national pride — shown in the friendly Ghana–Nigeria rivalry.
  • Serving: both are commonly served with chicken, fried plantain and salad.

Accurate differences include…

  • Ingredients (rice): Nigerian Jollof commonly uses long-grain parboiled rice; some Ghanaian cooks prefer aromatic jasmine or basmati.
  • Preparation: Nigerian party Jollof is valued for a smoky aroma from controlled heat and slight caramelization at the bottom of the pot.
  • Preparation / flavour: some Ghanaian cooks prefer a more aromatic, richly seasoned preparation.
  • Cultural context (serving): Ghanaian Jollof is often served with shito, kelewele or fried fish; Nigerian Jollof with moi moi or beef.
Check your own work
  • Is each similarity true of both traditions, according to this module?
  • Does each difference name a specific contrast — not just “they taste different”?
  • Did you label each one correctly as cultural context, ingredients or preparation practices?
  • Did you avoid treating a common pattern as a rule? (“Some Ghanaian cooks prefer…” is more accurate than “Ghanaians always…”.)

If any of your four statements does not meet these checks, revise it in the form above and submit again. Optional: share your comparison with a peer and discuss any differences you noticed.

Remember

Key takeaways

  • A strong comparison is specific and uses evidence from what you learned.
  • Similarities must hold for both traditions; differences should name a clear contrast.
  • Describe regional styles as common patterns, not fixed rules.
