1.3Traditional Dishes

Page 3 of 6 in Section 1
Orient

Orientation

A dish is a completed preparation. Here you will meet eight traditional dishes and the countries or communities they are most associated with.

Why it matters: Linking a dish to its context is half of the Classification Challenge.

Watch / Observe

Eight traditional dishes

For each dish, notice two things:
  • which staple it is built on (rice, cassava, yam, plantain, beans…)
  • the place or community it is associated with — shown in the gold tag

  • Jollof rice

    Several countries

    Rice cooked in a seasoned tomato and pepper-based sauce, usually with onions, oil and seasonings. Popular in Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal and The Gambia, and linked to celebrations.

  • Waakye

    Ghana

    Rice and beans cooked together, traditionally with dried sorghum leaves that give its colour. An everyday meal sold by vendors and made at home.

  • Thieboudienne (ceebu jën)

    Senegal

    Fish, rice and vegetables cooked together in a seasoned preparation. Significant in Senegalese food culture and communal dining.

  • Fufu and soup

    Ghana & Nigeria

    A dough-like food made from cassava, plantain, yam or combinations, served with soups such as groundnut, palm nut or egusi soup.

  • Egusi soup

    Nigeria & beyond

    A thick soup made with ground melon seeds, palm oil, leafy vegetables and protein. Especially popular in Nigeria and served with pounded yam, eba or fufu.

  • Groundnut soup

    Ghana & beyond

    A rich soup made with ground peanuts or groundnut paste, often with tomatoes, onions and meat or fish. Served with rice balls or fufu.

  • Akara

    Nigeria & beyond

    Fried fritters of ground beans (usually cowpeas) with onions and peppers. A breakfast food or snack, often with pap or bread.

  • Maafe

    Mali, Senegal & beyond

    A peanut-based stew of groundnut paste, tomatoes and onions, often with meat or fish. Commonly served with rice.

Some dishes belong to one country; others are enjoyed across several, often with differences in preparation.

Learn

Same staple, different dishes

Rice appears in Jollof rice, waakye and thieboudienne — but the three dishes have different ingredients, methods and cultural associations.

Cassava, yam and plantain are also used in different forms across the region, and names and methods change from place to place.

The lesson: recognizing a dish as West African does not mean it is made the same way everywhere.

Self-check

Quick check: dishes and context

Rice is used in Jollof rice, waakye and thieboudienne. What does this show?
Remember

Key takeaways

  • A dish is a finished preparation — it combines staples, ingredients and techniques.
  • Some dishes belong strongly to one place (waakye — Ghana; thieboudienne — Senegal); others, like Jollof rice, are shared across several countries.
  • The same staple can lead to very different dishes.
